## G2, and why the first measurement was wrong

| arm | inversions | claims | per claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| **agent** | **18** | 246 | 7.3% |
| baseline | 109 | 2,236 | 4.9% |

Per-meeting: agent fewer on 22, tied 13, worse 5. Judge `local:8090/gpt-oss-20b` (third
family — neither Qwen, which authored the references, nor Gemma, which translated the
corpus), 3 votes, **40/40 paired, zero judge failures**.

The first G2 run on this set reported 8 vs 11 on 19 paired meetings — nearly a tie. Two
independent defects produced that, both now fixed and pinned by regression tests:

1. **The pairing did not pair** (`metrics/stats.py::count_inversions`). It required the
   other arm to have *a record*, but ROUGE records and judge records are merged into one
   index and the ROUGE pass emits a record for every meeting in both arms — so every
   meeting looked paired even when only one arm was judged. 35 agent meetings were summed
   against 19 baseline meetings, giving "14 vs 11, FAIL". The fix requires the other arm
   to carry the inversions FIELD.
2. **The judge exhausted its budget in the reasoning channel** and returned empty
   `content` — 21 of 40 baseline meetings and 5 of 40 agent meetings. The losses were
   systematic, not random: failing baseline summaries had median length **5,087 chars
   against 562** for scored ones, so G2 was comparing only the control arm's SHORTEST
   outputs. The old retry re-sent an identical request to a `temperature=0` model, which
   reproduces the failure by construction. The retry now caps `reasoning_effort: low`.

After the fix: agent 35 → **40/40**, baseline 19 → **40/40**, zero failures. The measured
gap went from 8 vs 11 to **18 vs 109**. Judging only short summaries had concealed
essentially the entire effect.

## Caveats — these belong with any use of the table above

1. **G1 was trained for, not generalized to.** MeetingBank contains no within-meeting
   decision reversals: 3.4% of its 6,894 gold items match reversal language, and those
   matches are legislative boilerplate ("repealing Section 5.53.090") that reverses
   EXTERNAL ordinances. The capability was taught deliberately with synthetic data, so
   G1's two cases are no longer independent evidence. The independent 4-case probe
   (`data/reversals_probe`, scenarios sharing no subject, key term, domain or outcome
   vocabulary with training) went **2/4 → 2/4 — no detectable gain**. At n=4 it cannot
   distinguish a real capability gain from pattern match. **A larger independent probe set
   is the single most valuable next measurement.**
2. **G2's per-claim rate favours the BASELINE** (7.3% vs 4.9%). The agent wins on absolute
   inversions partly because it asserts 246 claims to the baseline's 2,236 — it says less,
   so it is wrong less often in absolute terms. SPEC §5.2's gate is the absolute count;
   both readings are true and both should be reported.
3. **G4 was never measured on the phone.** 19.58 min against a 20.00 ceiling is a 2.1%
   margin on a projection from 30-second bursts; sustained thermal throttling on a
   passively cooled device is unmodelled and acts in the failing direction. It is the only
   gate with no measurement behind it, and it is the number most likely to break first in
   the field.
4. **Two of the fixes that moved gates were mine, and both moved them favourably** — the
   pairing fix (G2 FAIL → PASS) and the retry fix (8 vs 11 → 18 vs 109). Both are correct
   on the merits and covered by regression tests, but they warrant outside scrutiny rather
   than being taken on trust.
5. Coverage and density favour the baseline, as they consistently have. They are
   diagnostics, not gates (SPEC §5), and measure length-correlated quantities against
   summaries that are ~8x shorter by design.
